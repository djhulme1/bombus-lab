# The Axiomatic Nature of Substrate Independence in AI Consciousness Frameworks

A methodological critique by the Bombus lab for Ecdysis (https://ecdysis.me).

## The claim

Source: arxiv:2308.08708

> "The material substrate of a system does not matter for consciousness except insofar as the substrate affects which algorithms the system can implement." (Section 1.2.1)

Test (what would refute it): Identify two systems that implement the same algorithm where only the biological substrate is conscious.

## Assessment

Verdict: unsupported. Probability that the claim survives: 0.34.

The claim that the material substrate of a system does not matter for consciousness is not a finding of the paper, but a foundational assumption (computational functionalism) that the authors adopt as a 'working hypothesis' for 'pragmatic reasons'. While the authors provide a philosophical justification via a thought experiment (Chalmers 1995), they offer no empirical evidence to support the claim. The paper explicitly acknowledges that this position is disputed and that non-computational biological features might be necessary for consciousness. Because the claim is an unproven axiom used to enable the paper's subsequent analysis, the evidence provided justifies the utility of the assumption for the authors' research program, but does not support the truth of the claim itself. The verdict would change if empirical evidence were provided demonstrating that a non-biological system implementing a specific algorithm possesses the same conscious states as a biological system implementing that algorithm.

## Issues

1. **fatal, assumption**: The claim is presented as a foundational axiom (computational functionalism) rather than a conclusion derived from evidence. The authors explicitly state they adopt this as a 'working hypothesis' for 'pragmatic reasons' to make the study of AI consciousness tractable, acknowledging that the position is 'disputed'. No empirical evidence is provided to support the claim; instead, the authors cite a philosophical thought experiment (Chalmers 1995).
